Birth & Death of Jesus Christ

This page presents Zetahir Mission’s own teaching on the birth of Jesus Christ, his mission of peace and unity, his crucifixion, and the call for mankind to approach his death with remorse, fasting and prayer.

According to the teaching of Zetahir Mission14 NovemberIs the true birthday of Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ’s True Birthday

Behold, a virgin shall be with a child and shall bring forth a son and they shall call him Emmanuel, which means God With Us (Matthew 1:23). The books written by Luke, Timothy, John clearly foretold the birth of Jesus Christ the Son of Man and his purpose sent by God/Allah (SWT). Over hundreds of decades after his birth humanity lost his birthdate and attached a Roman festival called Sol Invictus (the unconquered sun) to the birth of Christ. According to Zetahir's teaching, the true birthday of Jesus Christ is 14th November, revealed through Holy Prophetess Lehem (PBUH), rather than 25th December.

The mission’s teaching describes the date as a time connected with the rebirth of light, hope and spiritual awakening for mankind. It also teaches that the circumstances surrounding Jesus’ birth were shaped by threats against his life.

The True Mission of Jesus Christ

Jesus is presented as the Prince of Peace, sent by God/Allah (SWT) to bring salvation to humanity and reconcile the people with God/Allah (SWT) at a time when armed conflicts were rife. He came to teach unity, righteousness and brotherly love at a time of conflict and heal the sick. Jesus reads from Isaiah, saying "The spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim the gospel to the poor, heal the brokenhearted, preach deliverance to the captives and recover sight to the blind" (Luke 4:18). The book of John also shares light on the mission of Christ "For God sent not his son to condemn the world but that the world be saved through him. He that believed in him would not be condemned" (John 3:16-18). Jesus’ mission was, however, cut short by his untimely death.

The emphasis is peace: Jesus was not sent as a warrior. He came to teach peace, unity, righteousness and brotherly love.

The Crucifixion and Its Consequences

How did he die? Did he fall and die in the course of his teaching? Did he climb a tree and accidentally fall to his death in the course of imparting his knowledge to you? Or did he drown whilst crossing a river to another place in the course of his preaching? Or could it be that his death came as a result of exhaustion culminating in sickness and eventual death? For these reasons, you could be right in saying that he came to die for you. Zetahir Mission teaches that Jesus’ mission was cut short by his crucifixion. The mission does not present the crucifixion as a joyous event or as the purpose for which God sent Jesus. Instead, it teaches that Jesus was falsely accused, tried and killed, and that humanity should approach that event with sorrow and repentance.

The teaching refers to Pilate’s declaration of Jesus’ innocence and to the crowd’s demand for his crucifixion in Matthew 27:23–25. It describes the crucifixion as a grievous wrong against a messenger who had come to guide mankind.

According to Zetahir Mission, Jesus was crucified on 11 April. The date is therefore presented as a solemn day on which mankind should pray for forgiveness.

A solemn remembrance11 AprilPrayer · Fasting · Remorse · Forgiveness

A Call to Repentance

The mission teaches that there is no salvation in the act of crucifixion itself. Rather, God sent Jesus to reset mankind on the path of righteousness, and his work was brought to an abrupt end by people who opposed him.

Zetahir therefore calls on humanity to acknowledge what happened, show remorse and seek God’s forgiveness. Its message is not meant to glorify suffering, but to direct people back towards the purpose of Jesus’ mission: righteousness, peace, unity and a living relationship with God.

Zetahir Mission asks visitors to read this teaching with reflection and prayer, and to seek a closer relationship with the creator through truth, humility and upright living.
